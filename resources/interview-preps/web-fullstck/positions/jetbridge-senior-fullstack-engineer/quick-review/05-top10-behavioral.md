# Top 10 Behavioral Questions — Quick Review

> ⏱ ~10 min read | Use STAR format: Situation → Task → Action → Result

---

## 1. Tell me about your most significant technical achievement.

**What they want:** Impact + ownership. Pick your best project.

**Framework:** Describe the challenge → your architecture/solution → quantified business result.

> Think: What project am I most proud of? What metrics can I cite?

---

## 2. Describe a time you took full ownership of a feature end-to-end.

**What they want:** Autonomy, full-stack thinking.

**Framework:** Show you handled design → backend → frontend → deploy → monitoring without hand-holding.

> Think: When did I own something from zero to production?

---

## 3. Tell me about the hardest bug you've debugged.

**What they want:** Systematic problem-solving, not brute force.

**Framework:** Symptoms → investigation steps → root cause → fix → prevention measures.

> Think: What bug took me the longest? What did I learn?

---

## 4. How did you handle a disagreement with a teammate about a technical approach?

**What they want:** Conflict resolution without ego.

**Framework:** Show you proposed a fair evaluation (spike, data, team vote), acknowledged the other side's merits, and documented the decision.

> Think: REST vs GraphQL? State management choice? Testing approach?

---

## 5. Tell me about scaling a system to handle more traffic.

**What they want:** Production experience, cost awareness.

**Framework:** Original capacity → what changed → immediate fixes → medium-term architecture → result (throughput + cost).

> Think: When did something break under load? What did I do?

---

## 6. Describe a time you learned a new technology quickly to deliver.

**What they want:** Adaptability — JetBridge works with diverse clients.

**Framework:** What was new → how you learned (docs, prototypes) → what you built → timeline met.

> Think: LLM APIs? New framework? Cloud service?

---

## 7. Tell me about a mistake you made in production.

**What they want:** Accountability and learning culture. Don't dodge this.

**Framework:** What happened → how you responded → root cause → what you changed so it never happens again.

> Think: Bad deploy? Data loss? Outage? Be honest.

---

## 8. How do you communicate technical decisions to non-technical people?

**What they want:** Consulting skills — JetBridge works with clients.

**Framework:** Show you used analogies, visuals, or concrete examples instead of jargon. Show you listened to their concern first.

> Think: When did a PM/CEO/client challenge a tech choice?

---

## 9. Tell me about a time you proactively improved something without being asked.

**What they want:** Initiative and continuous improvement.

**Framework:** Noticed a problem → proposed a solution → implemented it → measurable improvement.

> Think: Slow CI? Bad PR process? Missing docs? Tech debt?

---

## 10. How do you handle changing requirements mid-project?

**What they want:** Flexibility without chaos.

**Framework:** Requirements changed → you had modular architecture → adapted without rewriting → delivered on time.

> Think: When did scope change? How did your architecture help?

---

## STAR Cheat Sheet

| Letter | What to say | Time |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **S**ituation | Context in 1-2 sentences | 10% |
| **T**ask | Your specific responsibility | 10% |
| **A**ction | What YOU did (be specific, use "I" not "we") | 60% |
| **R**esult | Quantified outcome (numbers, percentages, time saved) | 20% |

**Tips:**
- Prepare 3-4 stories that cover multiple questions each
- Always quantify results: "reduced by 40%", "saved 3 hours/week", "99.9% uptime"
- Say "I" not "we" even if it was a team effort — they want YOUR contribution
- It's OK to mention failures — what matters is what you learned
